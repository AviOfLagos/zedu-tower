A Zedu workspace you can walk around

Zedu Tower turns a chat workspace into an office building. Teams are floors, channels are rooms, and people are where their presence says they are. This page explains what you're looking at and how to test it.

What this is

A clickable prototype, built to test whether a spatial view makes it easier to see who's around, reach the right person, and join conversations without hunting through a sidebar. It runs entirely in the browser on sample data. Nothing is connected to Zedu yet.

You're always looking through one person's eyes: Avi, on F4 Engineering. Everyone sees the same building, but your view shows only your own conversations, and private rooms you're not in stay frosted. The camera, notifications and inbox are yours alone.

Works on computers and tablets. Phones get a "needs a bigger screen" message; chat on a phone stays in the regular Zedu app.

Two views

Tower

A side cutaway of the whole building. Good for "who's around and where". Drag to pan, scroll to zoom, click a room or a person to fly there. The left rail lists floors with a dot per person currently on them and a red dot where unread messages are waiting.

Floor plan

One floor from above, like an architect's plan: walls, doors, desks, meeting tables, lifts and stairs. Good for "who's in that meeting" and "who's sitting with whom". Open it with P, the Tower / Floor plan switch, or by clicking a floor in the rail twice. Both views run the same simulation, so people move in both.

Actions

Click any person, then use the buttons in the Details panel.

ActionWhat happens
Send messageDelivered instantly. Your courier bot then carries it to wherever they are, including the café, so you can see where it went and when it was read.
CallYou both pick up phones and a line links your offices. Nobody moves.
Walk overYou leave your desk, take the elevator and talk in person. The activity log tells you how long you were away.
Invite to huddleThey walk to your office. People in focus mode decline.
Join a meetingFrom the meeting card or the room: Join by audio (stay at your desk) or Walk in (take a seat).

How notifications work

  1. Messages are instant. The courier is decoration you can turn off with the Couriers button. Nothing waits for an animation.
  2. Only things that need you pop up: a message to you, an incoming call, a meeting starting. At most three cards, top right, each with its own buttons: Reply inline, Answer or Decline, Join by audio or Walk in.
  3. Everything else goes quietly to "Building activity" in the Inbox: calls ending, notes left on desks, people declining.
  4. Focus mode holds everything. Calls are declined and logged as missed. A "Focus · N held" counter sits in the top bar, and you get one summary when you switch back to Active.
  5. Off-screen people get an edge marker. If someone with unread messages, or someone you're on a call with, is out of view, a marker on the screen edge points to them. Click it to fly there.

What's real, what's mocked

Everything is sample data: 13 people, 8 floors, canned replies. The clock runs about 12 times faster than real time so walks and meetings don't take minutes. There's no login, no backend and no Zedu connection. Each visitor's browser runs its own copy, so two people testing at once won't see each other.

What's next

  • A bigger building: more, smaller rooms per floor, a conference hall floor, a cafeteria, a game room and a music room.
  • Different interior styles per floor, and larger meeting rooms where latecomers stand behind seated people.
  • Follow a person: the camera stays on them as they move.
  • Richer statuses ("On a call with Kemi", "Offsite", "Asleep") on hover and click, respecting privacy settings.
  • Hover hints that say what each floor, room and person is.
  • People walk through doors instead of cutting through walls.
